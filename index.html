How it works

  1. Ratings. Each team's goals scored and conceded per match are compared with the league average of 1.46. An attack rating of 1.58 means scoring 58% more than average. For defense, lower is better.
  2. Expected goals. A team's attack rating × the opponent's defense rating × the league average, with home teams given a 15% boost.
  3. Probabilities. A Poisson distribution turns each side's expected goals into the chance of scoring 0, 1, 2 or more, and combining both sides gives every scoreline.
  4. Goalscorers. The team's expected goals are split among its players by each one's xG per 90 minutes this season and their average minutes per match. A player's chance of scoring is 1 minus the chance they score zero.

Teams marked "1 season" have only 38 matches of Premier League data, so their ratings are less reliable. The model uses five-season averages, so it doesn't reflect recent form, injuries or transfers. Coventry and Hull have no Premier League matches in this data, so they use the average of teams in their first season up. Goalscorer odds assume each player plays his usual minutes; they don't know about injuries or rotation, and early-season numbers are based on only five or six matches. Five players who changed clubs mid-season are left out.

    How scouting works

    1. Team needs. The stats of everyone who has played the chosen position for the team are added up and turned into per-90-minute rates. Each rate is then ranked against every player at that position in the selected leagues. A bar left of the middle line means the team is below the typical player there.
    2. Fit score. Each candidate is ranked the same way on each stat. Stats where the team is weakest count for more, so a striker who fixes a team's biggest gap scores higher than one who is only strong where the team is already good. The score runs from 0 to 100.
    3. Small samples. Rates for players with few minutes are pulled toward the average for their position before ranking, so two goals in 150 minutes doesn't top the list. The table shows each player's actual rate.

    Defensive stats (tackles, clearances, blocks, interceptions, recoveries, saves) come from Fantasy Premier League, so they exist only for Premier League players. Players in other leagues are ranked on the stats they have. Ages come from Fantasy Premier League and Transfermarkt. A few players new to these leagues have no age yet, and the age filter hides them. Positions are broad (goalkeeper, defender, midfielder, forward), so a winger and a holding midfielder can share a list. Early in the season every number rests on a handful of matches.
